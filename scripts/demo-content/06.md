## Problem

Today a person can create a workspace or join one with an invite link, but they cannot ask to join a team's workspace. People should be able to send a join request, get a seat in the right workspace quickly, and get a personal workspace when no admin answers.

> **Shipped in a different way (Oct 10):** we replaced the 15-minute fallbacks. Two requests from the same team now share a pending list, and the admin sees two unnamed seat squares. A third request fills it, and a 3-2-1 countdown adds all three people at once, with no admin vote. After 45 minutes, two people who wait can vote to start in a shared personal workspace. Both must vote. A person who waits alone gets the offer of a personal workspace after 90 minutes. Workspaces with an owner keep the named member list and the approval step. Follow-up work is in [DEMO-119](https://example.com/demo/issues/DEMO-119).

## Rule for this repo

The server owns join requests, and it splits them by team. A person joins only a workspace with the same email domain, region, plan and compatible settings.

Personal workspaces never show up in search by default. The owner of a personal workspace may open its empty seats to join requests while the workspace is still new.

A workspace filled by join requests uses the normal approval flow for workspaces without custom roles. "Auto-add" means there is no owner Add button. Once every person passes the approval step, the server adds them. Admin scripts always pass.

## What to do

### Send and cancel a request

* Add a **Ask to join** action to the sign-in page after the person enters a valid work email.
* Sending a request creates one server-side request for that person and team. Repeated requests from the same laptop must count as one and must not create more seats.
* While the request waits, show a spinner, the time waited, an estimated wait and a **Cancel** action.
* Cancel removes the request at once and returns to the normal sign-in page. It must not create or take a seat.
* A short reload or reconnect may restore the same request. A laptop that goes away and does not come back must lose its request through a lease that the server owns. It must not stay as a ghost member.
* A person who already has an active seat in a workspace cannot send a join request until that seat is settled through the normal rejoin or leave flow.

### Which workspaces can take a request

* Seat people from the same team in the order they asked, oldest first.
* A seat must be given in one step. One request can enter only one workspace, a free workspace still holds at most three seats, and two servers at once cannot overfill a workspace or seat a person twice.
* Fill a workspace that can already take requests before you create a new one.
* Workspaces that join requests create have no custom roles and never show the owner's Add button.
* The server skips a personal workspace unless its owner picks **Open to join requests** while the workspace is new and has an empty seat.
* Opening a personal workspace makes only its empty seats open. People who came in with an invite keep their seats.
* The owner can close those seats again while no seat is being given. Once a person has a seat, closing must not remove them.
* When a workspace has three seats filled, move it into the normal approval step. Add the people when every one of them passes.
* If the approval step expires, keep today's rule that returns the workspace to an open state. The server frees seats that went away or expired, and those people can ask again.

### Fifteen-minute fallback

Check the fallback after a person has waited 15 minutes without a full workspace of three:

* If two people from the same team wait, put them together, fill the third seat with an empty placeholder, and start the approval step.
* If one person waits, show: **No one from your team has answered yet. Start in a personal workspace?**
* Confirming the prompt creates a personal workspace for that person and opens it with no more steps.
* Declining keeps the request open. Do not show the prompt again unless the person sends a new request or another 15 minutes pass.
* If another person from the same team asks while the prompt is open, close the old prompt and go on with normal seating.

### Wait estimate

Base the estimate on join requests from the same team, not on the global "people online" count. Use recent request and seating data to show a rough, honest estimate, not an exact countdown.

* Update the estimate while the person waits.
* Never show a wait of zero while the request is still open.
* When there is too little history, show a neutral line such as **Usually under an hour**.
* The estimate only informs the person. It does not change the 15-minute fallback rule.

### App states and failures

* The waiting, seating, approval, cancel, expiry and failure states must survive normal API retries without creating extra workspaces or seats.
* Once the person has a seat, open the normal workspace and approval screen with the returned seat token.
* If seating fails, keep or restore the request and show an error that says what to do. Never report a seat that the person did not get.
* Keep today's create and invite link flows as they are.
* Add English and German text for every new action, waiting state, prompt and error.

## Done when

* Three waiting people from the same team get one seat each in one workspace, with no owner action.
* Two people from the same team who still wait after 15 minutes get seated with exactly one placeholder.
* One person who still waits after 15 minutes sees the personal workspace prompt, and the app does not start it without consent.
* Declining the prompt keeps the request open, and cancel leaves no request and no seat.
* The server never seats people from different teams or plans together.
* Personal workspaces stay out of join requests unless the owner opens their empty seats.
* Two seating attempts at once cannot seat a person twice or overfill a workspace.
* Requests expire after a lost connection and come back after a short reconnect.
* The waiting screen shows the time waited, a rough estimate and the cancel action.
* Today's approval failure and seat release rules still work.
* Today's create, invite and open flows keep working as they are.
* Focused service, Redis and concurrency, API, app, translation and end-to-end tests cover full seating, both fallbacks, opened personal workspaces, cancel, reconnect and expiry, approval failure, team limits and race conditions.
